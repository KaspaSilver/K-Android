# K Android audit, 2026-11-25: battery first

**Why:** a user reports bad battery with K on Android.
**Code:** main ba052b76 plus the uncommitted port of iPhone 961fc05a and e4062188 (Explorer, wss, quantum Cold Storage, battery fixes, public chats page, KaPost threads, room deletes).
**Method:** six read-only reviewers (timers and loops; network, node pool and background; rendering, CPU and media; today's new code; notifications and money; chats, data and stability). The lead re-checked every High finding in the code. Their full write-ups follow as the appendix, with each reviewer's own IDs.
**Status, 2026-11-25 (night): every finding fixed in code, built, unit-tested, not yet committed or tried on a device.**

The fixes, in brief:

- **Fully fixed: 61 of 63.**
  - **A1:** the 1:1 sweep is 12 contacts on `ForegroundBeat`, 30 s on Wi-Fi and 60 s metered, twice that with push. The 2 s tick is for the open chat only (`ChatPollSchedule.kt`).
  - **A2:** the block stream runs only in the foreground while signed in. Bell-on rooms without push get a 60 s indexer poll in the background. Retries back off (`BlockStreamRetry.kt`).
  - **A3:** no background work when push is active, when signed out, or with notifications off (`PushState.backgroundQuiet`). Without push, the 1:1 loop does a light pass every 5 min that still reads the 12-contact window, so messages from existing contacts are still delivered.
  - **A4:** SyncWorker is scheduled only for accounts with groups while the background isn't quiet. Push registration is skipped when it is unchanged within 24 h on the same build.
  - **A5:** the QR scanner unbinds CameraX on close. There is one shared scanner now.
  - **A6:** the funding gate refreshes only while its screen is visible, on UTXO activity or every 30/60 s on the beat.
  - **Battery exemption:** asked for only when push can't deliver.
  - Every other finding: see the fixers' notes in the session.
- **Partly fixed: 2.**
  - **S13:** NextcloudSyncService is no longer built at startup. The remaining startup work is left as it was, because banner text needs it.
  - **S16:** queries rewritten, plus a new index (Room v46, `MIGRATION_45_46`). A separate column for the media-part tag is not done.
- **Unused strings removed:** `pc_new_public_chat_subtitle` and `pc_back` (left unused by the iPhone ef760cfa port, done the same night).

## The short answer

Android's battery problem is mostly not the iPhone's. iOS suspends K in the background, so its loops stop by themselves. Android keeps the process alive, and K asks every user for the battery-optimisation exemption, so Doze doesn't stop it either. Several loops that are fine while K is on screen keep running for hours with the screen off. On top of that, the 1:1 chat poll is about ten times busier than the iPhone's while K is open.

## Battery, Medium

- **Automatic Scan stuck "unhealthy":** a full-wave probe of ≈40–50 endpoints every 10 s, possibly for good (N8; High for Automatic Scan users).
- **Unaligned timers:** about ten foreground timers on their own phases, so the cellular radio never reaches idle. The 1:1 loop, Address Activity and the trusted-node keepalive aren't on `ForegroundBeat` (B3, N9).
- **Address Activity:** polls each spending address one by one every 30 s, even with the feature off (B7).
- **Group pushes:** every group push runs a full catch-up of every group in the push handler (N10, S6).
- **Push registration:** redone on every cold process start, background wakes included: 2 HTTPS calls plus signing (N3).
- **Background network change:** a change or "back online" while in the background redials and pins the node, bypassing the push pause (N4).
- **Block-stream STOP:** a NOTIFY_START that times out or is cancelled can leave the node streaming blocks with no STOP ever sent (N5).
- **Background close runs once:** anything reopened later in the background stays open (N6).
- **Push state restored late:** the loops that check it start first, so a background wake can run a full probe and a 1:1 sweep (S7).
- **1:1 tick repeats work:**
  - payment and token notices start a new chain of on-chain checks on every tick (S3);
  - alias discovery downloads 25 full transactions per contact every 5 min, every 30 s for the open chat (S4);
  - the "one-time" stuck-send repair scans every message on every tick (S5).
- **Open group thread:** reloads and re-maps its whole history on every group write; the decrypt cache thrashes past 4,000 messages (S9).
- **Spinners and fee shimmer:** redraw at full display rate (60–120 Hz) and keep running in hidden Hub sections (R4).

## Battery, Low

- **Signed out:** the node pool keeps probing (N12, B6).
- **Pushes:**
  - a dead pinned node is retried at full cadence forever (N13);
  - payload-less DM pushes retry the REST read for up to 13 s per part (N11).
- **Background CPU:**
  - the .kachat registration driver and the unnamed sweep wake in the background (B9, S14);
  - screen-scoped timers keep waking in the background (B10);
  - collectors aren't lifecycle-aware, so the shell's list and badge queries run in the background (R6).
- **Hidden screens:**
  - KaPost's "new posts" check runs every 60 s while hidden in the Hub (R3);
  - the send screens' BlockDAG keeps drawing after "Sent" (R5);
  - the Explorer's live dot recomposes ~20 times a second (R9);
  - the dock re-blurs the page under a 92 % opaque tint (R10).
- **Media:**
  - GIFs play twice at once in the full-screen viewer (R7);
  - Nextcloud voice bubbles prepare a MediaPlayer on scroll (R8).
- **Small repeated work:**
  - two small database writes on every 1:1 tick (S15);
  - incoming reactions and edits are re-written inside the rewind window (S11);
  - hot chat queries scan the whole wallet on every message write (S16);
  - the deleted-message log is rewritten in full on each delete (S17);
  - cold start does Keystore and disk work for about 15 services on the main thread (S13).

## Correctness

Nothing Critical or High. No problems were found in send amounts, fees, change, unknown outcomes, quantum cold sends, kUSDT, Swap, or how keys and secrets are stored and logged.

- **Medium, P1:** Delete Account in Settings, with another account still saved, leaves that account half signed in. The screen shows Welcome, but push registers for it, its banners show, its loops run, and the next cold start opens it without the biometric prompt.
- **Low, today's code (T1–T7):**
  - quantum Max/Compound with over 32 coins can price and build different coins;
  - a quantum change address goes back to the pool after an "outcome unknown" broadcast;
  - two missed indexer reads can come from one push;
  - a room delete also removes a row whose content is exactly "edit" or "reaction";
  - history keeps the address list from the first open;
  - a delete for everyone ignores an account switch mid-send;
  - manual wss nodes are stored by the typed text.
- **Low, push and sends (P2–P6):**
  - an unreadable group push while the app is open gets no banner;
  - a cancelled send is reported as failed, not unconfirmed;
  - serial push handling can run past FCM's window;
  - four channels are all named "Messages";
  - an unknown-outcome payment notice is sent again (one extra message fee, no double payment).
- **Low, data (S10, S12):**
  - concurrent `syncGroups` can send duplicate backfills;
  - pull-to-refresh racing the poll can reset read state.

## Suggested order

1. **A2, A3, A4:** the screen-off drains.
   - Stream blocks only in the foreground.
   - In the background without push, skip the contact sweep, or hand it to one WorkManager job.
   - Enqueue SyncWorker only without push and with groups.
   - Re-register push only when something changed.
2. **A1:** a 1:1 sweep of 12 contacts every 30 s (60 s metered) on the shared beat, measured from the sweep's end; 2 s ticks for the open chat only, as on the iPhone.
3. **A5, A6:**
   - unbind the camera on dispose;
   - poll the funding gate only while resumed and visible, ending it on the existing UTXO-activity signal.
4. **The rest:**
   - N8, B7, N10/S6, S3–S5, S7, R4;
   - P1;
   - the Lows.
5. **Then:** only ask for the battery exemption when push is unavailable.

## Findings

### B1 The 1:1 poll reads every contact every 15 s, and nearly non-stop while any chat or group is open
**Severity:** High
**Where:** repository/ChatRepository.kt:150-212 (loop), :2306-2335 (sweep gate, cap 40), :2391-2440 (per-alias pages), :2554-2557 (own sends 60 s), :2242 (alias discovery), :4717-4760 (constants); services/NotificationHelper.kt:280-282 (`isChatOpen` is true for an open **group** too)
**What:** Android has no live UTXO subscription for 1:1, so this loop is the only live path, and its contact sweep runs whenever 5 s have passed since the last one, which is every tick:
- Idle on Wi-Fi (15 s tick): every 15 s, up to 40 contacts × 1-3 alias GETs, plus own sends of each contact every 60 s (1-2 GETs), alias discovery every 5 min per contact (a REST call returning 25 full transactions, then a decrypt try per unknown alias), 2 handshake GETs per tick, the inbox every 30 s and payments every ~30 s. With 20 contacts at 2 aliases that is about 160 + 30 + 4 + 8 + 2 + 2 ≈ **200 requests a minute** on the Chats tab.
- In a chat on Wi-Fi (2 s tick): a 20-contact sweep is ~40 serial requests plus 19 × 110 ms spacing, about 5-8 s. `lastContactSweepAt` is set at the start, so the next tick 2 s after it is already past 5 s and sweeps again: the sweep runs back to back for as long as the chat is open. Add 30 handshake ticks a minute and the open chat's own incoming and outgoing reads every tick: **about 400-500 requests a minute**.
- Opening a **group** sets `isChatOpen`, so the 1:1 loop goes to the same 2 s tick and back-to-back sweep, with no 1:1 thread on screen (`currentContactId` is null).
- Metered: 30 s idle / 5 s in a chat, and the sweep still runs on every tick.
- iOS (KaChat/Services/ChatService+Conversations.swift:275-395, ChatService.swift:377-386): the same sweep is a backstop, every 30 s on Wi-Fi / 60 s on cellular, 12 contacts in a rotating window, and it stands down while the UTXO subscription is healthy.
**Why it matters:** this is the largest foreground cost in the app: the radio and TLS stack are busy almost continuously while K is open, and on cellular the radio never reaches idle. It is roughly 6× iOS's whole steady state (≈33 requests/min) on the Chats tab, and 15× in a chat.
**Fix:** 
1. Keep the 2 s tick only for the open 1:1 conversation (its incoming and outgoing reads).
2. Run the all-contacts sweep on `ForegroundBeat` (30 s / 60 s) with a rotating window of about 12 contacts, as iOS does.
3. Read handshakes once per sweep, not every tick.
4. Base `isChatOpen` for this loop on `currentContactId` only, so an open group doesn't speed it up.
5. Move own sends of closed chats to every 5 minutes.
6. In the long run, a UTXO subscription on the chatting address would let the sweep stand down as on iOS.
**Confidence:** Confirmed (request counts are estimates from the code, not measured).

### B2 Bell-on rooms keep a full block stream open in the background on Wi-Fi, with the screen off and after logout
**Severity:** High
**Where:** services/BroadcastScanningService.kt:396-420 (`reevaluate`), :113-117, :437-470; repository/BroadcastRepository.kt:1079-1103 (`noteIndexerResult`), services/database/BroadcastDao.kt:80 (bell = always-listen); services/NodePoolManager.kt:1626 (background close skips connections with block subscribers)
**What:** `stream = if (metered && !foreground) emptySet() else rooms.filter { !hasIndexerDelivery(room) || … || (!metered && !foreground && !pushState.isActive && room in alwaysListen) }`, where `rooms` is every bell-on room. On Wi-Fi in the background the node pushes every block (~10 a second) while any of these holds:
- a bell-on room the indexer doesn't track (a room the user made or joined that isn't curated and isn't indexer-listed);
- a bell-on room whose indexer was marked unreachable (3 IOExceptions or timeouts within 2 minutes; `meansDown` counts a timeout). The mark clears only on a later answer, and in the background nothing asks: the sweep and the room poll are foreground-only. So one flaky-Wi-Fi moment before going to the background keeps the stream on for the whole background period;
- the arena channel with its indexer down, if its bell is on;
- **every** bell-on room when push is inactive (notifications switched off, testnet, registration failed).

The background close in NodePoolManager keeps this connection open (it has a block subscriber), with its HTTP/2 keepalive. `getAlwaysListenChannelNames` is keyed on `activeAddressFlow`, which survives logout, and `reevaluate` never checks sign-in, so the stream also runs signed out. iOS never streams in the background (it is suspended).
**Why it matters:** a socket receiving about 10 blocks a second (tens of KB/s, roughly 1-4 GB a day per the code's own comment) keeps the Wi-Fi radio active and wakes the CPU for every packet, even with the screen off. While the process isn't frozen that means hours of awake CPU and radio: the worst drain in the app. It also runs every other background loop (B4), because the phone never sleeps. ### N1 Full-block stream runs in the background on Wi-Fi (room bells), all night if exempt

**Severity:** High

**Where:** BroadcastScanningService.kt:388-421 (`reevaluate`, the `stream` filter at :408-412), :220-228 (`hasIndexerDelivery`), :434-466 (retry loop, `RETRY_DELAY_MS` 5 s at :701); BroadcastRepository.kt:1081-1105 (`noteIndexerResult`), :1425 (push fetch skips a down indexer); NodePoolManager.kt:1628 (background close skips a connection with block subscribers).

**What:** `reevaluate()` gates only the metered case on foreground (`if (metered && !foreground) emptySet()`). On Wi-Fi, a bell-on room keeps the NOTIFY_START block stream **while the app is in the background, screen off included**, in three cases:
1. `!hasIndexerDelivery(room)`: the room isn't indexer-tracked, **or its indexer was marked down**. Being marked down takes three failures inside two minutes. The mark is cleared only by a successful indexer answer (`noteIndexerResult`), and nothing asks the indexer in the background. The closed-room sweep is foreground-only (:261-276), and `fetchBeforePushBanner` returns at once for a down indexer (BroadcastRepository.kt:1425). So an indexer blip just before the phone is locked keeps the stream on until the app is next opened.
2. `!pushState.isActive && room in alwaysListen`: **every** bell-on room when push isn't active. That covers no Play services, K or Android notifications off (see N8), and a failed registration.
3. A cold process started in the background by FCM or SyncWorker builds BroadcastScanningService at startup (KaChatApplication.kt:244), so cases 1 and 2 start the stream in that process too.

The node sends every full block with verbose data, about 10 a second. The code's own estimate is 1-4 GB/day. Every block is protobuf-decoded and its transactions scanned, even though most rooms never match. If the node is down, the loop redials every 5 s plus the RPC timeout, with no backoff and no online check. `enterBackground()` never closes this connection, because it has block subscribers.

**Why it matters:** Wi-Fi packets arrive every ~100 ms, so the CPU and the Wi-Fi radio never idle. Expect on the order of 36,000 block messages and roughly 40-170 MB per hour, continuously, for as long as the phone sits locked on Wi-Fi. That is tens of percent of battery overnight. With the exemption granted (the app asks for it), Doze doesn't stop it. This is the Android twin of iOS B1, and it is worse here: iOS is suspended in the background, so it never had a background stream.

**Fix:** Smallest: make the stream foreground-only. In `reevaluate`, use `val stream = if (!foreground) emptySet() else rooms.filterTo(...)`. Background bell-on rooms are then covered by push, or by the existing 60 s background-without-push pace. If the background no-push room delivery must stay, poll the room's indexer at 60 s instead of streaming. In addition:
- re-check a down indexer in the background with one cheap request every few minutes (like `IndexerHealth.claimRecoveryCheck`);
- give the 5 s retry loops (Broadcast, Group, inbox scan, KaPostsChainFallback) a doubling backoff and an online wait.

**Confidence:** Confirmed (code path). The data rate is the code's own estimate.

### N4 A network change or "back online" in the background re-dials the node and pins it, bypassing the push pause

**Severity:** Medium

**Where:** NodePoolManager.kt:467-500 (callback), :507-538 (`onNetworkPathChanged`), :541-547 (`onBackOnline`), :549-566 (`resetForNewNetworkPath`, which calls `refreshNow()`), :1492 (`refreshNow` has no background gate), :1118-1127 (`syncPinnedConnections`); KaspadConnection.kt:397-430 (a pinned connection reconnects forever).

**What:**
- In the background with push, `enterBackground()` closes the node connections and the probe loop waits at its gate.
- The default-network callback keeps running, though. On a Wi-Fi/cellular switch, a VPN change or coming back online, `onNetworkPathChanged()` finds no connection to ping, and `resetForNewNetworkPath()` calls `refreshNow()`. That runs `probeCycle(force = true)` straight away, with no foreground/push check.
- In trusted mode (the default), that is `connectionFor(trusted)`, which opens a new gRPC/TLS channel. `syncPinnedConnections()` then pins it, so it reconnects forever with up to 30 s backoff whenever the stream drops.
- In Automatic Scan it can also start the DNS and wss walks.
- `onBackOnline()` does the same through `reconnectStaleConnections()` plus `refreshNow()`.
- Nothing closes these connections again until the app comes to the front (see N6).

**Why it matters:** Phones change default network several times a day in a pocket: leaving Wi-Fi, a lift, a VPN on-demand. After each change, a backgrounded K holds an open TLS/HTTP2 socket with HTTP/2 PINGs every 20 s (Wi-Fi) or 45 s (metered), `keepAliveWithoutCalls(true)`. A carrier NAT idle timeout kills it, and the pinned reconnect redials with a new TLS handshake. The timers can't wake a sleeping CPU, so the cost lands whenever the device is awake for anything else, plus every redial. This is exactly what B3 (the background close) was meant to stop.

**Fix:** In `onNetworkPathChanged` / `onBackOnline`, when `!notificationHelper.isAppInForeground && pushState.isActive`, only reset state:
- `probeEpoch++`;
- `forgiveFailures()`;
- close the connections;
- clear `lastDnsResolveAt` and the blocked flag.

Skip `refreshNow()` and `reconnectStaleConnections()`. The return to the foreground already reconnects (KaChatApplication.kt:410) and the probe loop runs at once.

**Confidence:** Confirmed.

### N5 A NOTIFY_START that times out or is cancelled can leave the node streaming blocks with no STOP ever sent

**Severity:** Medium

**Where:** KaspadConnection.kt:534-558 (`subscribeToBlockAdded` adds the owner before the request and never removes it on failure), :568-588 (`unsubscribeFromBlockAdded` sends STOP only when the set is empty); BroadcastScanningService.kt:440-445 and :469-484; GroupScanningService.kt:237-242 and :272-287; ChatRepository.kt:4254-4290 (`subscribed` set only after success); NodePoolManager.kt:1568-1595 (`subscribedOn` set only after success); NodePoolManager.kt:1628.

**What:**
1. The owner is added to `blockSubscribers` before the NOTIFY_START request.
2. If that request times out (5 s) or the coroutine is cancelled mid-flight, the request may still have reached the node. Cancellation mid-flight happens exactly when the app goes to the background, or a room screen closes, while a subscribe is in flight.
3. The callers record the connection only after success (`subscribedConnection = conn`, `subscribed = true`, `subscribedOn = connection`), so their stop paths send nothing.
4. The owner stays in the set for that stream generation. Every other owner's later `unsubscribeFromBlockAdded` then sees a non-empty set and **suppresses the STOP**.
5. `hasBlockSubscribers` stays true, so `enterBackground()` keeps the connection open.

KaPostsChainFallback (KaPostsChainFallback.kt:645-680) already does this correctly: it assigns `conn` before subscribing and always unsubscribes in `finally`.

**Why it matters:** When it hits, the outcome is iOS B1: about 10 full blocks a second decoded by gRPC (the shared flow drops them, but deserialisation already happened). The connection and stream survive the background with push until the stream dies or the app is reopened. That can be hours on Wi-Fi. The race window is one NOTIFY_START round trip, so it is rare, but the cost is large when it hits.

**Fix:** In `subscribeToBlockAdded`, wrap the call. On any exception or cancellation, remove `owner`, and if the set is now empty, send NOTIFY_STOP under `NonCancellable` with a short timeout. Make the four callers assign the connection before subscribing, as KaPostsChainFallback does.

**Confidence:** Confirmed (code). How often the race hits is Plausible.

### N6 The background idle-connection close runs once; anything reopened later in the background stays open

**Severity:** Low-Medium

**Where:** NodePoolManager.kt:1614-1636 (`enterBackground`); the stop paths at BroadcastScanningService.kt:469-484 and GroupScanningService.kt:272-287 launch their NOTIFY_STOP asynchronously; KaspadConnection.kt:196-211 (`keepAliveWithoutCalls(true)`).

**What:**
- The close of idle connections for the background (push active) happens once, at the transition.
  - It races the scanners' stop paths: they remove their owner inside a launched coroutine. If `enterBackground`'s loop reaches the primary first, `hasBlockSubscribers` is still true and the primary is kept. Nothing re-checks after the STOP lands.
  - Any connection opened later in the background is never closed. Openers: N4, N1, an FCM- or worker-woken process that sends (group backfill sends, KNS reveal resume, scheduled KaPost sends).
- All gRPC channels use `keepAliveWithoutCalls(true)`, and the MessageStream is a permanently open call anyway. Every such channel pings every 20 s (Wi-Fi) or 45 s (metered) for the rest of the background period.

**Why it matters:** A small but unbounded background radio cost, plus reconnect TLS handshakes when NATs drop the idle socket. It also undermines the B3 fix in exactly the cases where the user just left a room or the Explorer.

**Fix:**
- When the last block client leaves (unsubscribe last-out) while backgrounded with push, close that connection unless requests are pending.
- More generally, in the background with push, close any connection that has had no request for ~30 s, using a single small sweep that runs only while connections exist.

**Confidence:** Confirmed (code). The race order is Plausible.

### N8 Automatic Scan can sit in "unhealthy" mode for good: full-wave probe of ~40-50 endpoints every 10 s

**Severity:** Medium (High for Automatic Scan users)

**Where:** NodePoolManager.kt:169 (`targetActiveNodes = 8`), :178 (10 s), :600-634 (cadence), :886-929 (DNS/wss jobs and the full wave while `activeCount < 8`), :960-967 (prune Quarantined discovered/DNS), :985-1021 (peer gossip every unhealthy cycle); KaspadConnection.kt:124-142 (`isGossipedNodeAddress` accepts any port).

**What:** While fewer than 8 nodes are Active, every cycle:
- probes the full wave: 6 seeds, up to 20 gossiped, 20 DNS answers, 3 wss seeds, manual and stand-ins, each with its own new ManagedChannel;
- re-resolves 7 DNS seeds every 60 s;
- walks the wss resolvers (cooldown 2-15 min);
- runs `GetPeerAddresses` (often 10-20 KB).

The cycles run every 10 s in the foreground and every 120 s in the background without push. Failed endpoints are closed and redialled from scratch the next cycle.

Why the pool can stay below 8:
- `GetPeerAddresses` returns peers' P2P addresses (port 16111), and DNS seeders return P2P nodes, which mostly don't expose gRPC (rusty-kaspa's rpclisten defaults to localhost).
- Gossiped entries fail, are Quarantined after 3 cycles (30 s), pruned, then re-added by the next gossip, because the pruned set isn't remembered.
- The dead-cycle backoff only applies when *nothing* answers. One live node keeps the 10 s cadence forever.

**Why it matters:** In the foreground on cellular, about 40-50 connection attempts every 10 s keep the radio permanently in high power. There is also steady CPU for channel setup and teardown (a gRPC transport per endpoint, TLS for wss), and a full-wave pass with DNS every 2 min in the background without push.

**Fix:**
- Back the unhealthy cadence off when the Active count didn't rise over the last cycle: 10, 20, 40, then a 60 s cap, reset when it rises or on a path change.
- Accept gossiped addresses only on the gRPC port (or map 16111 to 16110).
- Remember pruned addresses for ~1 h so gossip can't re-add them.
- Consider "healthy" at min(8, a reachable estimate), like iOS's conservative mode.

**Confidence:** The loop is Confirmed. That real pools stay below 8 is Plausible (not measured on a device).

### N9 Trusted-mode keepalive: two liveness mechanisms, neither on the shared beat

**Severity:** Low-Medium

**Where:** NodePoolManager.kt:600-610, :818 (`trustedKeepaliveMillis` 30 s Wi-Fi / 45 s metered), :834-847 (skips only on a block notification); KaspadConnection.kt:178-183, :196-211 (HTTP/2 keepalive 20 s / 45 s, `keepAliveWithoutCalls(true)`); ForegroundBeat.kt.

**What:** In the foreground with the default pinned node, two things check liveness:
- the probe loop sends GetInfo + GetBlockDagInfo every 30 s (45 s metered), timed from the end of the previous cycle;
- gRPC sends its own HTTP/2 PING 20 s (45 s) after the last read.

On Wi-Fi that is a wake at about +20 s and +30 s of each 30 s window. On cellular, the 45 s probe/PING drifts against ForegroundBeat's wall-clock 60 s beat, which the room sweep, KaPost, kUSDT and the group pass all use. **Why it matters:** About 2.3 radio wakes a minute on cellular instead of 1, so the LTE tail (about 10 s) is paid 2-3 times a minute. This is the remainder of iOS B4.

**Fix:**
- Sleep the trusted probe with `ForegroundBeat.sleepUntilNextBeat(offset)` while in the foreground.
- Set the gRPC `keepAliveTime` at or above the probe interval (60-90 s), because the probe already detects dead sockets.
- Skip the probe when any response arrived within the interval (track `lastResponseAtMs` in `handleIncoming`).

**Confidence:** Confirmed (code). Radio-tail numbers are estimates.

**Where:** KaChatFirebaseMessagingService.kt:577-604 (`handleGroupMessagePush`), :736-755 (`handleGroupControlPush`), :1012-1018 (budgets 8 s / 13 s / 18 s); GroupRepository.kt:2575-2657.

## Steady-state cost summary

**App open (default pinned node, Wi-Fi, idle):** One gRPC/TLS connection, with a GetInfo + GetBlockDagInfo probe every 30 s and an HTTP/2 PING in between (about 4 node wakes a minute). Add the ForegroundBeat polls and a CPU-only public-fallback check every 20 s. There is no block stream unless the Explorer, an open room or an uncovered bell-on room wants one. On cellular, the 45 s probe and PING drift against the 60 s beat (N9). In Automatic Scan the cost can jump to about 40-50 connection attempts every 10 s if the pool never reaches 8 Active (N8).

**App in background or screen locked, push active (typical Play user):** The node pool pauses and closes its idle connections. The 1:1 poll pauses. In the best case the remaining costs are:
- SyncWorker every 15 min, through Doze when exempt (N2);
- a push re-registration on each cold wake (N3);
- a full group catch-up per group push (N10).

Several leaks reopen the node connection and leave it pinging until the next foreground: any network change (N4), a lost NOTIFY_START (N5), and the one-shot close (N6). Worst of all, an untracked bell-on room, or a room indexer that blipped before the phone was locked, keeps the full block stream (about 10 blocks a second) running on Wi-Fi for the whole time the phone is locked (N1).

**Background without push (no Play services, or notifications off, or signed out, N7):** A 60 s indexer sync, 120 s node probes with the connection held, and on Wi-Fi the full block stream for every bell-on room (N1). For an exempt app that is the dominant overnight drain, and it applies even to users who turned K's notifications off.

## Steady-state cost (this area)

### S1 The 1:1 poll fans out to every contact on almost every tick while the app is open
**Severity:** High
**Where:** repository/ChatRepository.kt:140-217 (loop), :1723-1797 (`syncMessages`), :2307-2333 (sweep gate), :2335-2470 (per-contact fetch), :2560-2614 (outgoing), :4717-4760 (constants)
**What:**
- Android has no utxosChanged subscription for 1:1 chats. So the indexer poll is the live path, and every tick does all of this:
  - `syncHandshakes` and `syncOutgoingHandshakes`: 2 GETs per tick, the outgoing one included. iOS treats the outgoing read as a bootstrap step.
  - The contact sweep: every alias of up to 40 contacts (`CONTACT_SWEEP_CAP`), 110 ms apart. It runs whenever 5 s (`CONTACT_SWEEP_MIN_INTERVAL_MS`) have passed since the last sweep **started**.
  - Own sends: each contact every 60 s.
  - Payments every 20 s and the inbox every 30 s.
- Tick spacing is 2 s with a chat open (Wi-Fi), 15 s idle (Wi-Fi), and 5 s / 30 s on cellular.
- A 20-contact sweep takes about 2 s of spacing plus about 40 round trips, so about 6-10 s. That is longer than the 5 s gate, so **with a chat open every tick is a full sweep, back to back**.
- iOS runs its contact sweep only while the UTXO subscription is unhealthy: a window of 12, every 30 s (60 s on cellular). See K-iPhone/CLAUDE.md "Foreground contact sweep" and ChatService+Conversations.swift:354-388.
- The 1:1 loop is also not on `ForegroundBeat`, the shared beat the group, KaPost and kUSDT loops now use. So it wakes the radio on its own phase.

Estimates:

| State | Requests/min |
|---|---|
| Chat open, Wi-Fi | ≈ 300 |
| Chats tab idle, Wi-Fi | ≈ 140 |
| Idle, cellular | ≈ 90 |

iOS's whole app idles at about 33/min.

**Why it matters:**
- The cellular radio never reaches idle while K is open. On cellular, about 10 s of each 30 s cycle is sweep traffic plus the roughly 10 s LTE tail.
- On Wi-Fi the radio and CPU are busy almost all the time.
- Each response is parsed and every row checked against Room (`existsShown`). This is the largest foreground cost in this area, likely in the app.

**Fix:**
- Smallest change: make the sweep its own cadence, 30 s on Wi-Fi and 60 s metered, aligned to `ForegroundBeat`.

- Cap a pass at the 4 most recent contacts plus a rotating slice (12 in all, as iOS does).
- Read incoming handshakes once per sweep, not per tick. Read outgoing handshakes only on full syncs (`!fromPollLoop`).

**Confidence:** Confirmed (code path). The rates are estimates.

### S5 The "one-time" stuck-send repair scans every message of the wallet on every tick
**Severity:** Medium
**Where:** repository/ChatRepository.kt:1259-1277 (called at :1734 at the top of every `syncMessages`); services/database/MessageDao.kt:201-209
**What:**
- `repairStuckProvisionalMessages` runs before the indexer gate on every poll tick: 2 s with a chat open, 15 s idle, 60 s in the background without push.

**Fix:**

### S9 An open group thread loads and re-maps its whole history on every group write; the decrypt cache thrashes past 4,000 messages
**Severity:** Medium
**Where:** repository/GroupRepository.kt:369-386 (`getMessages` with no window), :465-477 (LRU of 4,000, access order); services/database/GroupDao.kt:49-50; ui/screens/GroupChatScreens.kt:274
**What:**

  - Every startup step and lifecycle callback is guarded. Foreground coroutines rethrow CancellationException.
  - MainActivity strips consumed link, share and notification extras, so a recreate does not replay them. Share copies accept only content:// and are try/catch guarded.
  - No GlobalScope. No Activity context is held in singletons: AppText/KaPlayText keep the application or base context.
  - FCM's `runBlocking` runs on the FCM worker thread, not the main thread.

## Steady-state cost (this area)
- **App open:**
  - The 1:1 indexer poll dominates: about 140 requests/min idle on Wi-Fi, about 90 on cellular, about 300 with a chat open, as continuous back-to-back contact sweeps (S1). Alias discovery adds full-transaction downloads (S4), and each received payment notice briefly adds a burst (S3).
  - Every tick also runs a whole-wallet message scan (S5) and a write (S15). Group polling is beat-aligned and modest.
  - An open long group re-decrypts its history on each write (S9).
- **Backgrounded with push:**
  - In-process loops mostly pause, but each process start (every push when the process was dead, and SyncWorker every 15 min) runs heavy main-thread startup (S13). It may also run one full probe cycle and one full 1:1 sweep before the push state is restored (S7).
  - Every group push runs a catch-up of all groups (S6).
  - SyncWorker wakes the app 96 times a day even though push covers groups (S8).
- **Backgrounded or screen off without push** (no Play services, failed registration, all testnet):
  - The 1:1 loop keeps doing a full contact sweep every 60 s, about 70 requests/min, for as long as the OS leaves the process unfrozen, with the battery exemption the app requests (S2).
  - The .kachat sweep timer also wakes every minute (S14).
- **Fix order:** S1, S2 and S7 are the largest and simplest wins; S3, S5, S6 and S8 follow.
